## Talking points

### The setup
- Two harnesses, kept strictly apart: one for work, one for personal projects. Same process, different tools.
- The team lives outside the code: each personal repo's harness (roles, decisions, state, reviews) sits in a private repo and is linked in as `teammates/`. The public repo never sees it.
- `/meio-pm` starts a session: it finds the harness, reads state, reconciles it against git, and reports "where are we and what's next".

### The team
- PM (my single point of contact), Architect, Developer, QA, Reviewer, and a Game Designer as the judge of "good".
- Builders and checkers come from different model families, so nobody grades their own homework.
- Grill first: every topic or build starts with one question at a time.

### One process
- Write (with Uncle Bob's ub-mode discipline) → independent review → a gate that must be green on the latest commit → I see it working.
- The gate: checks, QA and a review with zero important findings, all pinned to the exact commit. Any new commit resets it.
- Round three with findings means stop patching: fix the whole class of bug or rebuild.

### I don't read the code
- Where I land between "stop reading AI code" and "keep reviewing, in layers".
- I judge by playing. Agents check agents. Machines check the boring stuff.
- Real catches from one afternoon that tests missed: a ladder that could float forever and lock everyone off the roof; late joiners freezing for seconds; one client able to get everyone else kicked.

### Standards without reading code
- A written code standard: what blocks a merge vs what's a nit. Strict on structure, not style.
- My architecture preferences: dependency injection, layers by responsibility, events, tests per layer.
- Domain-oriented naming: one product glossary shared by me and the code.

### The game
- Cake Contractors: tiny contractors decorating a giant cake in a 1950s kitchen, for coworkers who don't play games.
- Two rival companies: PuildBass (a cheerful bass) vs Crowpour (a too-perfect corporate crow).
- Roblox-style controls because a non-gamer teammate already knows them.

### What I'd tell past me
- Judge ideas by what they catch and what they cost; add one at a time.
- It's okay not to understand every concept you throw at agents.
- Keep the human job small and clear: decide, play, accept.
